Research Interest

Active directions I am shaping into research questions.

This section is intentionally more detailed than the homepage. Use it for the ideas you are currently reading, testing, proving, discussing, or preparing to turn into archived drafts and future manuscripts.

Core direction

Multi-Armed Bandits and exploration strategy

Study how a learner should allocate trials across uncertain actions when every choice gives information about only one arm. This is the foundation for thinking about exploration, exploitation, regret, and sequential decision quality.

  • Questions: Which arm should be tried next, and why?
  • Methods to compare: UCB-style optimism, Thompson sampling, and elimination ideas
  • Useful outputs: simulations, regret plots, and concise theorem notes
Preference feedback

Dueling Bandits and pairwise comparison learning

Explore settings where feedback comes from comparisons rather than scores. Instead of asking "how much reward did this action get?", the learner asks "which of these two options is preferred?"

  • Questions: What can be learned from noisy pairwise preferences?
  • Objects to define: preference matrix, Condorcet winner, regret notion
  • Next step: connect assumptions to realistic human-feedback settings
Sequential setting

Online Learning and adaptive decision rules

Build intuition for algorithms that update after each observation. The goal is to understand how decisions compound when information arrives gradually and the environment may not be perfectly still.

  • Questions: How fast should the learner adapt?
  • Focus: regret, robustness, changing feedback, and repeated interaction
  • Current artifact: reading summaries and small experiment scaffolds
Human signal

Preference Learning from rankings, clicks, and choices

Connect bandit feedback to human preference data. The same system may receive comparisons, implicit clicks, rankings, or selected options, and each signal has its own bias and uncertainty.

  • Questions: What does the observed preference really mean?
  • Risk: treating noisy human feedback as a clean mathematical oracle
  • Useful output: a taxonomy of feedback types and assumptions
Theory layer

Learning Theory for guarantees and limitations

Use mathematical analysis to understand when a learning rule can be trusted, how much data it needs, and where its assumptions may fail. This is where intuition becomes a careful statement.

  • Questions: What can be guaranteed, and under which assumptions?
  • Tools: concentration, regret decomposition, lower/upper bounds
  • Current artifact: proof sketches and assumption checklists
Research practice

Reproducible simulations and archived notes

Keep experiments, reading maps, and draft notes organized from the beginning. The goal is to make future papers easier to verify, explain, and extend.

  • Questions: Which baselines, metrics, and plots make the idea clear?
  • Artifacts: notebooks, experiment logs, literature tables, draft PDFs
  • Reminder: if future-you cannot reproduce it, future-you will object politely

Concepts

The research area, without assuming the visitor already speaks bandit.

01

Multi-Armed Bandits

Imagine several options, each with an unknown payoff. The learner chooses one option, observes only that result, and must decide what to try next. The core tension is exploration versus exploitation.

Small translation: the algorithm is learning by poking the world, politely.

02

Dueling Bandits

Sometimes users cannot give a reliable score, but they can compare two options. Dueling bandits learn from pairwise preferences such as "A is better than B" instead of numeric rewards.

Useful when stars, ratings, and vibes refuse to be the same thing.

03

Online Learning

Online learning studies systems that receive data sequentially and update decisions over time. The learner cannot wait for a perfect dataset; it must act, learn, and correct itself as evidence arrives.

The model is basically doing homework while the exam is already happening.

04

Preference Learning

Preference learning asks how to infer what people or systems value from comparisons, rankings, clicks, selections, or other indirect signals. It connects naturally to recommender systems and human-AI feedback.

05

Learning Theory

Learning theory gives the mathematical language for asking when an algorithm should work, how much data it needs, and how much regret or error it may accumulate along the way.

06

Why They Fit Together

Bandits define the feedback structure, online learning defines the sequential setting, preference learning defines the signal, and learning theory helps explain what can be guaranteed.

Decision Loop

The common pattern underneath the work.

Most projects on this page can be described as a loop: choose an action, receive limited feedback, update a belief, and choose again.
