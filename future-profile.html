About Me

Academic foundation, professional ambition, and a research practice built to evolve.

I am a PhD student in [program/department] at [university], working on Multi-Armed Bandits, Dueling Bandits, Online Learning, Preference Learning, Sequential Decision Making, and Learning Theory.

My research mission is to understand how algorithms should explore, compare, learn preferences, and make reliable decisions under uncertainty. In plain language: I study how to make good choices before the universe has finished explaining itself.

Area of Research

Learning by trying, comparing, and adapting.

My work studies decision-making systems that must learn from partial feedback: choosing actions, comparing alternatives, and improving policies while uncertainty remains.

Multi-Armed Bandits

Pick an arm, observe a reward, learn the best choice.

Each pull gives only partial feedback, so the algorithm must learn from experience.

Exploration vs Exploitation

Should we test a new option or use what seems best?

Move the control to see the decision policy shift.

Dueling Bandits

Learn from pairwise preferences instead of numeric scores.

Two actions are compared; the winner updates the learner's belief about preference.
