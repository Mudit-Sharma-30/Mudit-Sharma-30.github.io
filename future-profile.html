About Me

Academic foundation, professional ambition, and a research practice built to evolve.

I am a PhD student in [program/department] at [university], working on Multi-Armed Bandits, Dueling Bandits, Online Learning, Preference Learning, Sequential Decision Making, and Learning Theory.

My research mission is to understand how algorithms should explore, compare, learn preferences, and make reliable decisions under uncertainty.

Research Interests

Learning by trying, comparing, and adapting.

My work studies decision-making systems that must learn from partial feedback: choosing actions, comparing alternatives, and improving policies while uncertainty remains.

Multi-Armed Bandits

Pick an arm, observe a reward, learn the best choice.

Each pull gives only partial feedback, so the algorithm must learn from experience.

Exploration vs Exploitation

Should we test a new option or use what seems best?

Move the control to see the decision policy shift.

Dueling Bandits

Learn from pairwise preferences instead of numeric scores.

Two actions are compared; the winner updates the learner's belief about preference.

Research Theme

Multi-Armed Bandits and Sequential Decision Making

I study how learners allocate trials across competing actions, balancing information gathering with reward-seeking over time.

Current Project

Dueling Bandits and Preference Feedback

Learning from comparisons when users can say which option they prefer, but not assign absolute scores.

  • Focus: pairwise comparisons, preference noise, regret
  • Output: algorithms, guarantees, and empirical evaluation
Ongoing Work

Online Learning Under Uncertainty

Designing algorithms that adapt as data arrives, decisions compound, and environments change.

  • Focus: adaptive policies, regret analysis, robustness
  • Status: active research direction
Future Direction

Learning Theory for Preference-Driven Systems

Building theoretical tools that explain when preference-learning systems can make reliable decisions from sparse, comparative data.
